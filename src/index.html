Many teams already run their work in Jira but keep their customers in a spreadsheet or a separate CRM. Dealtrail CRM puts the sales side next to the delivery side: you track who might buy, what you are selling and what to do next, and you can point any record at the Jira work item that delivers it.

How the pieces fit

The app has one pipeline and a handful of record types. Each links to the others, so you can move from a deal to its contact, its company and its tasks without searching.

  1. Leads are people or companies that might buy. You rate them, work them and either convert or lose them.
  2. Deals are sales you are working on. They move through the stages of your pipeline and carry an amount, a close date and a next step.
  3. Contacts and companies are who you sell to. A contact belongs to a company, and a company can have a parent.
  4. Products are your catalogue. Attach them to a deal and its amount follows.
  5. Tasks and notes record what to do and what happened, on any record.
  6. Dashboard and reports turn all of it into numbers: what needs attention now, and how a period compares with the last one.
  7. Jira work items connect it all to delivery: a work item shows its customer and deal, JQL can find work by CRM data, and Automation rules can update the CRM.

Where to open it

Open Dealtrail CRM from the Apps menu in the Jira sidebar. The app has one global page with a menu on the left, in four groups: Sales (Leads, Deals, Contacts, Companies, Products), Work (Tasks, Activities), Insights (Dashboard, Reports) and Workspace (Settings). The arrow at the top of the menu collapses it to a column of icons, and the app remembers that for you alone.

The app also adds a CRM panel to every Jira work item, four read-only fields for search, a dashboard gadget and Automation actions.
